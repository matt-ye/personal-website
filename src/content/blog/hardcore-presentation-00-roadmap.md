---

## 為什麼用「職能」這個視角

我的本業是職能治療與職能科學。在這個領域裡，**「職能」（occupation）指的不是職業，而是一個人在特定情境下，所從事、且對他有意義的活動**。煮一頓飯、彈一首曲子、上台做一場簡報——這些都是職能。

職能治療有一個經典的分析框架：**PEOP 模型（Person–Environment–Occupation–Performance）**。它的核心主張很簡單，卻常被簡報書忽略：

<div class="callout">
  一個人的<strong>表現（Performance）</strong>，從來不是單靠「個人能力」決定的，而是<strong>人、環境、職能本身三者交互作用</strong>的結果。
</div>

換句話說，一場簡報講得好不好，不只看講者的口條。它同時被「台下是誰、場地多大、設備如何」（環境），以及「這是學術報告、產品 pitch，還是婚禮致詞」（職能本身的要求）所決定。**把這三個面向拆開來看，比起籠統地說「要練台風」，更能定位問題到底出在哪。**

這就是這個專欄的骨架。

PEOP 模型最重要的提醒是：**表現（最後一格）是前三格交互作用的結果，不是一個可以單獨苦練的東西。** 當一場簡報失敗時，與其只檢討講者，不如回頭問：是人的狀態（緊張到忘詞）？環境的錯配（投影片在大場地看不清）？還是職能定義錯誤（把產品 pitch 講成了技術論文）？

## 這個專欄會怎麼寫

每一篇都會走同一條路徑，照顧不同深度的讀者：

1. **先給實用技巧**——一個能直接套用的做法
2. **再說應用場景**——這招在什麼情況下用、什麼情況下別用
3. **接著解釋為什麼**——背後的原理
4. **最後補文獻與機制**——想深究的人，可以順著參考資料往下挖

對多數讀者來說，**ARE（Assertion–Reason–Evidence，主張–理由–證據／例子）** 的順序最好吸收：先給結論，再給理由，最後才是細節證據。這個專欄本身，也會用這個結構來寫。

並且，因為內容會大量引用學理，這裡立三條規矩：

<div class="callout">
  <strong>① 尊重智慧財產權</strong>：他人的圖表、教材、案例，註明出處或重新製作，不直接挪用。<br>
  <strong>② 事實查核</strong>：每個「研究顯示……」都要能追到原始文獻，不轉述二手懶人包。<br>
  <strong>③ 標示不確定</strong>：學理有爭議、或我只是合理推論的地方，會明講，不假裝定論。
</div>

## Why look at it through "occupation"

My own field is occupational therapy and occupational science. In that field, **"occupation" doesn't mean a job — it means an activity a person engages in, in a particular context, that carries meaning for them**. Cooking a meal, playing a piece of music, giving a presentation: all occupations.

Occupational therapy has a classic analytic framework for this: the **PEOP model (Person–Environment–Occupation–Performance)**. Its central claim is simple, and routinely overlooked by books on presenting:

<div class="callout">
  A person's <strong>performance</strong> is never determined by individual ability alone. It is the product of <strong>the interaction between the person, the environment, and the occupation itself</strong>.
</div>

Put another way: how well a presentation goes isn't only about the speaker's delivery. It is equally shaped by who is in the room, how large the venue is, what the equipment is like (the environment) — and by whether this is an academic talk, a product pitch, or a wedding speech (what the occupation itself demands). **Separating these three does far more to locate the actual problem than a general instruction to "work on your stage presence."**

That is the skeleton of this column.

The most important thing the PEOP model insists on is that **performance (the last box) is the result of the first three interacting, not something you can drill in isolation.** When a presentation fails, rather than only interrogating the speaker, go back and ask: was it the person's state (too nervous to remember the lines)? A mismatch with the environment (slides illegible in a large hall)? Or a misdefined occupation (delivering a product pitch as a technical paper)?

## How this column is written

Every piece follows the same path, so readers at different depths are served:

1. **A usable technique first** — something you can apply directly
2. **Then the situation** — when to use it, and when not to
3. **Then why it works** — the underlying principle
4. **Finally the literature and mechanism** — for anyone who wants to dig further

For most readers, the order of **ARE (Assertion–Reason–Evidence)** goes down easiest: conclusion first, then the reason, then the supporting detail. This column is written in that structure too.

And because the content leans heavily on published research, three rules are set here:

<div class="callout">
  <strong>① Respect intellectual property</strong>: other people's charts, materials and cases are attributed or redrawn, never lifted directly.<br>
  <strong>② Check the facts</strong>: every "studies show…" has to be traceable to the original paper, never relayed from a summary.<br>
  <strong>③ Flag uncertainty</strong>: where the science is contested, or where I'm only reasoning plausibly, I say so rather than pretending it's settled.
</div>

## In short

- The aim of this column is to turn the tacit knowledge of presenting — the part I use but can't explain — into something explicable, grounded in theory
- The map is occupational therapy's **PEOP model**: performance = the interaction of person × environment × occupation
- Every piece runs in the same order — **technique → situation → why → literature** — matching the ARE structure
- Intellectual property, fact-checking and citation are respected throughout

Next we begin at the outermost ring, the **Environment (E)** — background research before a presentation: which physical, social, cultural and political cues you should read before you open your mouth.
