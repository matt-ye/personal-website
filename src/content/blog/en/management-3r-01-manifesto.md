This is the opening piece of **Right Time, Right Place, Right People: A 3R Field Guide for Modern Managers**.

This column is a management classroom I've opened for myself — to properly understand the management ideas I come across, then file them in a framework solid enough to search and revisit later. It is written for the version of me who wants to step up into leading a team, and to lead it well.

---

## Why a framework is needed at all

Management offers a dizzying number of tools: OKRs, KPIs, PDCA, SWOT, lean production, agile development. Memorising them one by one means forgetting them one by one. What actually accumulates is a set of drawers high-level enough that every tool has somewhere to go.

The drawers I've chosen are the three terms every Chinese speaker knows — **天時, 地利, 人和** (*tiān shí, dì lì, rén hé*). Rendered in English as **Right Time, Right Place, Right People**, they escape the narrow framing of "human resources" and cover the three core faces of modern management: **strategy, operations, and organization**.

---

## The 3R framework: three drawers

### 1. Right Time (天時) — strategy and change management

Everything to do with the external environment and the dimension of timing.

- **Strategic timing**: when to enter a new market, when to ship a new product (market validation in [agile development](https://agilemanifesto.org/) and [the lean startup](https://theleanstartup.com/principles)).
- **Change management**: when to transform the organisation — while the company is making money, or once a crisis hits? (See [Kotter's eight steps](https://hbr.org/1995/05/leading-change-why-transformation-efforts-fail-2).)
- **Crisis management**: making decisions inside the window that matters.

### 2. Right Place (地利) — operations, process and resource allocation

Everything to do with internal space, structure and resources.

- **Structure**: how are departments divided? How far is authority delegated? (Matrix organisations, flat hierarchies.)
- **Operations**: putting capital, equipment and technology where they generate the most value ([lean production](https://www.lean.org/explore-lean/a-brief-history-of-lean/), supply-chain management).
- **Physical and digital space**: balancing remote work against office culture.

### 3. Right People (人和) — culture and human capital

Everything to do with people, psychology and teams.

- **Talent acquisition**: selection, evaluation and retention, so each person's strengths get used ([Gallup's strengths-based management](https://www.gallup.com/cliftonstrengths/en/253754/history-cliftonstrengths.aspx)).
- **Collaboration and [psychological safety](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/understand-team-effectiveness)**: building a high-trust culture and cutting the friction between departments.
- **Leadership**: how a manager motivates, sets a vision, and gets everyone pulling the same way.

## The whole thing as one matrix

| Dimension | English label | Face of management | The core question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 天時 | Right Time | Strategy and change | *When* do we act? (timing and trends) |
| 地利 | Right Place | Operations and structure | *Where* do we act? (process and resources) |
| 人和 | Right People | Organisation and culture | *Who* acts? (talent and collaboration) |

The appeal of the framework is that any management tool ([OKRs](https://www.whatmatters.com/articles/the-origin-story), KPIs, [PDCA](https://deming.org/explore/pdsa/), [SWOT](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024630123000110)) and any new business case can be dropped into one of the three drawers without strain.

## A second axis for retrieval: eight business functions

3R is the vertical axis — the management lens. A company's actual operation runs on a horizontal axis of **business functions**. Chinese management textbooks most often teach five (production, marketing, human resources, R&D, finance), frequently adding information systems for a sixth. For ease of retrieval, this column expands that to eight:

> **Marketing · HR · Production · Finance · R&D · General affairs · IT · Management**

Every article is filed in one of the 3R drawers and also tagged with the function it belongs to. That way you can navigate by level of thinking (Right Time / Place / People) or by concrete work context (the function) — and cross-reference the two.

## References and further reading

Most of the concepts above have a traceable origin. They are grouped below into the framework itself and the methodological tools.

**The framework itself**

- 天時, 地利, 人和 — from *Mencius*, Book of Gongsun Chou II: "The right time is less than the right place; the right place is less than people in accord." This column borrows its three-part structure and maps it to Right Time / Right Place / Right People.
- Business functions (production, marketing, HR, R&D, finance) — the standard classification in Chinese management teaching, often extended with information systems. See [Asia University's summary of the five functions](https://ba.au.edu.tw/p/404-1043-63549.php) (in Chinese). The eight-way split is this column's own expansion for retrieval, not a standard term.

**Methodological tools**

- Agile — the [Agile Manifesto](https://agilemanifesto.org/) (Kent Beck and 16 others, 2001).
- The Lean Startup / Build-Measure-Learn — Eric Ries, [*The Lean Startup*](https://theleanstartup.com/principles) (2011).
- Change management — John P. Kotter, [Leading Change: Why Transformation Efforts Fail](https://hbr.org/1995/05/leading-change-why-transformation-efforts-fail-2) (HBR, 1995; the book *Leading Change*, 1996).
- Lean production / the Toyota Production System — pioneered by Taiichi Ohno; popularised as "Lean" by Womack and Jones. See [Lean Enterprise Institute: A Brief History of Lean](https://www.lean.org/explore-lean/a-brief-history-of-lean/).
- CliftonStrengths — Don Clifton and Gallup, [History of CliftonStrengths](https://www.gallup.com/cliftonstrengths/en/253754/history-cliftonstrengths.aspx).
- Psychological safety — proposed by Amy C. Edmondson (1999) and borne out by Google's Project Aristotle, see [Google re:Work — Understand team effectiveness](https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/understand-team-effectiveness).
- OKRs — originating with Andy Grove at Intel and popularised by John Doerr (*Measure What Matters*). See [What Matters — The Origin Story](https://www.whatmatters.com/articles/the-origin-story).
- PDCA — proposed by Walter Shewhart and developed by W. Edwards Deming, [The Deming Institute — PDSA Cycle](https://deming.org/explore/pdsa/).
- SWOT — generally credited to Albert Humphrey and SRI (Stanford Research Institute); on its murkier origins see the review [The origins of SWOT analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024630123000110).
